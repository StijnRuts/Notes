# Digital garden

## About

A digital garden is a personal website or knowledge space where notes and ideas are published at different stages of development,
organized by connections rather than chronology, and allowed to evolve openly over time.
Unlike a blog's reverse-chronological "stream", a garden favours topography: notes are linked by theme and concept, and older notes remain as valuable as new ones.
The term was popularized by Mike Caulfield in 2015 and expanded by Maggie Appleton, whose six patterns describe
navigation by topic, continuous growth, learning in public, personal experimentation, diverse content, and independent ownership.
Notes are often labelled as seedlings, budding, or evergreen to signal their maturity.
